# Over 600 students learnt "Proof of Work" during BlockchainFUL24 in Nigeria

Last month, I had the privilege of being a Keynote speaker and panelist at the BlockchainFUL Conference 24 in Lokoja, Kogi State, Nigeria — and it was one of the most energising rooms I have been in all year.

Over 600 students showed up. Not just showed up — they engaged, challenged, and asked exactly the right questions.

## What the conference was about

BlockchainFUL is an initiative focused on blockchain education within university communities in Nigeria. The goal is simple and necessary: demystify the technology, connect students to real builders and practitioners, and give the next generation of Nigerian engineers and entrepreneurs an honest picture of what building with blockchain actually looks like.

The 2024 edition in Lokoja brought together speakers from across the crypto and blockchain space in Nigeria and beyond. Panels covered everything from technical fundamentals to regulation, policy, and the practical realities of building in this environment.

## What I spoke about

My keynote centred on the concept of **Proof of Work** — borrowed deliberately from the Bitcoin consensus mechanism, but applied to how individuals build credibility, capability, and careers in emerging technology.

The argument: in a space as noisy as crypto and blockchain, the only thing that cuts through is demonstrable, compounding work. Not talk. Not titles. Not the number of conferences you attend. But what you have actually built, shipped, broken, and rebuilt.

I talked about my own path — what I have learned building Monierate, what the blockchain space in Nigeria actually looks like from inside it, and what I wish someone had told me earlier.

## The students

What struck me most was not the number — 600 is impressive — but the quality of engagement.

These were students from Kogi State and surrounding institutions who had clearly done their own reading, their own thinking. They pushed back on narratives that deserved to be pushed back on. They asked pointed questions about regulation, about how to actually get jobs in the space, about what is real and what is hype.

That skepticism is healthy. The Nigerian blockchain ecosystem does not need more believers. It needs builders who can think critically and still choose to build.

## Why this matters

Education bottlenecks are real in the Nigerian tech space. The gap between what is taught in universities and what is needed in the industry is wide. Events like BlockchainFUL exist in that gap, trying to bridge it.

600 students who now have a clearer picture of blockchain technology, its applications, and its limitations — that compounds. Some of them will build companies. Some will go into policy. Some will write. All of them will carry this knowledge somewhere useful.

That is what the work is for.
