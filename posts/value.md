# Value

Value can be in two forms: instant and usable, or perceived and maybe useful.

Most people fall into the trap of confusing the two. And most builders — products, companies, even personal careers — are built on one while pretending to offer the other.

## Instant and usable

This is value that works the moment you encounter it. It solves the problem in front of you right now. The currency exchange tool that gives you the rate. The medication that reduces the fever. The sentence that answers the question. No explanation required. No time investment demanded. Value delivered, received, and integrated.

Instant value has a clarity to it. The user knows they got something. The builder knows they delivered something. The transaction is clean.

## Perceived and maybe useful

This is value that requires belief before it can function. It is the supplement that *might* improve your health over years. The course that will *eventually* make you more productive. The network you are building that will *someday* open doors.

Perceived value is not fraudulent by nature. Many things that require time and belief before they pay off are genuinely valuable. The equity you take instead of salary. The relationship you invest in long before it becomes useful. The skill you build in obscurity.

The problem is when perceived value is sold as instant value. When the timeline is hidden. When the "maybe" is removed from the pitch.

## The market sorts this over time

Short cycles reward perceived value. Attention is cheap to buy, at least initially. But over longer cycles, the market is brutally honest. Products that solve real problems survive. Products built on sustained perception without underlying utility eventually collapse.

The companies and individuals who understand this build differently. They ask: does this actually work? Not: does this look like it works?

## The personal implication

This distinction matters for how you build your career, not just your product.

You can spend years accumulating the perception of expertise — the titles, the network, the speaking appearances — without building the underlying competence that makes expertise real. This works until it does not. And when it stops working, there is nothing beneath it to stand on.

The more honest path is to build real utility first. To become someone who solves real problems with genuine skill. The perception, when it comes, is durable because it is attached to something true.

---

Ask yourself, about whatever you are building right now: is this instant and usable? Or is it perceived and maybe useful?

Both can be valuable. But only if you are honest about which one you are offering — and so is your user.
