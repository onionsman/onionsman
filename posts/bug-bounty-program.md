# Why Every Tech, Fintech, and Bank Needs a Bug Bounty Program — Immediately

Recent cybersecurity incidents across Nigeria underscore a critical need for proactive security measures within the Digital Asset and broader financial services industry.

We are past the point where "we take security seriously" is a sufficient public statement. The question now is: what are you doing about it, structurally and continuously?

## The problem with reactive security

Most companies in the Nigerian tech ecosystem operate on a reactive security model. A breach happens. Investigators are called. Patches are applied. A statement is issued. The cycle repeats.

This model is broken. It is expensive, reputationally damaging, and fundamentally backwards. You are always one step behind an adversary who has already found the door and walked through it.

Bug bounty programs flip this dynamic. Instead of waiting for bad actors to find your vulnerabilities, you invite good-faith researchers — ethical hackers, security engineers, and curious builders — to find them first and report responsibly.

## What a bug bounty program actually is

A bug bounty program is a structured, public or private agreement that offers financial rewards (bounties) to individuals who discover and responsibly disclose security vulnerabilities in a company's systems, applications, or infrastructure.

It is not a replacement for internal security teams. It is a multiplier. It crowdsources the offensive security intelligence of hundreds or thousands of skilled eyes across the globe, focused on your specific systems.

Platforms like HackerOne, Bugcrowd, and Intigriti facilitate these programs at scale. Many companies run their own private programs directly.

## The case for Nigerian fintech specifically

The fintech space in Nigeria is moving at a pace that regularly outstrips the security investment behind it. New products launch weekly. APIs are exposed. Mobile apps are shipped. Payment flows are built on top of third-party rails with varying security postures.

The attack surface is enormous and growing. The talent pool of external researchers who want to test these surfaces — and get paid for doing it responsibly — is also growing.

Digital Asset companies face an additional layer of risk. The irreversible nature of blockchain transactions means that a successful exploit does not result in a recoverable situation. Money moved is money gone. This is not like a traditional bank fraud case where reversals and investigations can recover funds. The stakes are categorically higher.

## What a program needs to work

A bug bounty program is only as good as the culture behind it. Three things matter most:

**Clarity on scope.** Researchers need to know exactly what is in and out of bounds. Vague scope invites confusion and creates legal exposure for researchers who may test things you did not intend.

**Fair and fast payouts.** If researchers do the work and wait months for payment, word spreads. Your program becomes irrelevant. Speed and fairness build trust and attract better talent.

**A genuine commitment to fixing what is found.** Programs that receive reports and deprioritise fixes are missing the point entirely. The bounty is the cost of admission. The fix is the product.

## The reputational argument

Beyond the security benefits, there is a reputational argument that is often overlooked. Running a public bug bounty program signals something important to your users, your partners, and your regulators: that you are not waiting to be caught. You are actively seeking your own weaknesses before someone else exploits them.

In a market where trust is everything and history is littered with breaches that destroyed companies, this signal matters.

---

The companies that survive the next decade of Nigerian fintech and digital asset growth will not be the ones that were never attacked. They will be the ones that built systems robust enough to withstand attacks and cultures honest enough to invite scrutiny.

Start the program. Start it now.
