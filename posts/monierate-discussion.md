# A discussion about Monierate progress, future outlook and Blockchain regulation in Nigeria

The team at CryptostreetTV reached out and wanted an exclusive and in-depth conversation on everything Monierate has done to achieve over 200,000 monthly visitors, where we are headed, and what blockchain regulation in Nigeria actually looks like from the inside.

It was one of the more honest conversations I have had publicly about this journey.

## Where Monierate started

Monierate was built to solve a simple, persistent problem: Nigerians had no clean, reliable, real-time source of truth for exchange rates across platforms. The parallel market operates differently from the official rate. Different platforms — fintech apps, bureaux de change, crypto exchanges — all price differently. Getting an accurate comparison required manual effort and often resulted in bad decisions.

We built Monierate to remove that friction. One place. Real-time rates. Transparent comparisons.

## What 200,000 monthly visitors actually means

The number gets cited often, but I want to put it in context. 200,000 monthly visitors, completely bootstrapped, post-revenue, one year after launch — that is not a vanity metric in this case. It represents genuine demand for what we built.

These are people making real financial decisions. They come to Monierate to answer a real question before moving money. When the product works well, they make a better decision than they would have otherwise. That is the measure we actually care about.

Getting here required not just building the product but understanding distribution — how Nigerians search for this information, where they look, what language they use, and what trust signals matter to them before they act on a rate.

## The regulation conversation

Blockchain regulation in Nigeria has been one of the most volatile and evolving conversations in African fintech over the last several years. The CBN has moved in multiple directions. Crypto was effectively banned, then unbanned, then regulated. The SEC has been developing a framework. The conversation is genuinely in flux.

My view, which I shared in the interview: the direction of regulation matters less than the predictability of it. Builders can work with almost any regulatory environment if it is consistent and clearly communicated. What kills innovation is ambiguity — rules that change without notice, enforcement that feels arbitrary, and no clear path to operating legitimately.

Nigeria needs a regulatory framework for digital assets that is both protective (of consumers, of the financial system) and enabling (of innovation, of legitimate businesses). These goals are not in conflict. Other jurisdictions have found ways to hold both simultaneously.

## Future outlook

For Monierate, the roadmap is clear: deeper utility. We are not just a rate tracker — we are building toward becoming the most useful financial comparison tool in Nigeria and eventually across Africa.

The rates are the entry point. What users need beyond that is context, guidance, and tools that help them not just find the best rate but act on it efficiently and safely.

That is the work ahead. And the foundation — the traffic, the trust, the data — is already being built.
