# African Bitcoin Day 2024

Words are just words when results do not convert into action. I was at Africa Bitcoin Conference 2024 with a keynote session — and that line stayed with me through most of what I saw and heard over those days.

## What the conference represents

The Africa Bitcoin Conference has become one of the most important gatherings for the Bitcoin ecosystem on the continent. It brings together builders, investors, educators, policymakers, and curious minds from across Africa and the global Bitcoin community. The energy is different from other crypto conferences — it is less speculative, more philosophical, more grounded in the immediate realities of living in economies that Bitcoin was, in many ways, designed to address.

Inflation. Currency devaluation. Limited banking access. Capital controls. These are not abstract concepts for most attendees — they are daily realities that give Bitcoin's promise a very concrete shape.

## What I was there to say

My keynote was built around one core idea: the gap between Bitcoin's potential on the African continent and its current adoption is not a technology problem. It is an education, trust, and usability problem.

The protocol works. The infrastructure is increasingly available. What is missing, in too many communities, is the knowledge and confidence to use it — and the onramps that make that use genuinely accessible.

The work that closes this gap is not glamorous. It is teaching, building tools, creating content, hosting workshops, answering questions that have already been answered a hundred times for a hundred different people. It is slow and largely invisible work. And it is the work that actually moves adoption.

## The room

What strikes me every time I attend gatherings like this is the quality of the builders. Young engineers from Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Cameroon — many of them working on products most of the world has not heard of yet, solving problems most of the world does not know exist.

That is where the real story of Bitcoin in Africa is being written. Not at the conference podium. In the code and the conversations happening around it.

## After the words

The keynote went well. The conversation continued over the following days. But I keep coming back to that line: words are just words when results do not convert into action.

The question I left with — the question I am still working on — is what action, specifically, does this moment require from me?

For now, the answer is: build more. Write more. Show more of the real work.
