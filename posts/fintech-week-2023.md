# Hosted Over 8,000 People at Fintech Week 2023

Getting over 8,000 people interested enough to learn and share insights about Fintech in Nigeria is not something that happens by accident. It is the result of months of deliberate work, good timing, and the growing appetite of a market that knows the financial system it lives in needs to change.

Fintech Week 2023 was one of the most significant things I have been involved in building. Here is what the experience actually taught me.

## Why fintech events matter in Nigeria

The Nigerian financial services sector is undergoing a genuine transformation. The traditional banking system, for all its entrenchment, is being disrupted — not by startups in a distant Silicon Valley but by builders who live with the problem every day. Builders in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, who have experienced the friction, the fees, the exclusion, and decided to fix it.

Events that gather these builders — alongside the regulators, the investors, the consumers, and the curious — accelerate something that is hard to quantify but easy to feel: the sense that a community exists, that the work is connected, that the sum is larger than its parts.

That is what Fintech Week was designed to do.

## 8,000 people — what that actually means

8,000 is a number. What it represents is more interesting.

These were people who cleared their schedules, travelled in some cases, sat through sessions, asked questions, connected with each other. In a country where attention is finite and competing demands are constant, that level of commitment to a topic signals genuine interest.

The conversations at the margins of events — the ones that happen in hallways and over meals, the introductions that turn into partnerships, the ideas that get pressure-tested by someone you just met — that is where a lot of the real value of gatherings like this lives.

## What I learned from hosting

Logistics at scale are humbling. Everything that can go wrong will consider going wrong. The preparation is never quite enough. You learn to build redundancy into everything and still be ready to improvise.

But more than logistics: I learned that what people actually want from events is not lectures. They want to be in the room with people who are doing real things. They want access, proximity, honest conversation. The best sessions were not the ones with the most polished presentations — they were the ones where speakers talked about what actually happened, what failed, what surprised them.

## The fintech moment in Nigeria

Nigeria's fintech sector is at an inflection point. Regulation is evolving. Infrastructure is improving. The talent pool is deep. Capital, while more cautious than it was in 2021, is still present.

The ecosystem needs events that serve the ecosystem — not just the most visible companies or the most funded startups, but the full range of people doing the work of transforming how 200 million people interact with money.

Fintech Week was one attempt at that. There will be more.
