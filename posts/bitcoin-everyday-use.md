# How to use Bitcoin and blockchain for everyday use in Nigeria and Africa

My friend Kelechi called me and asked: "Would I be interested in sharing my thoughts and experience on Bitcoin and blockchain technology for everyday Nigerians?" The answer was yes — because this question gets asked constantly, and the answers given are usually either too technical or too vague to be useful.

This is an attempt at a practical answer.

## Start with the actual problem you are trying to solve

Bitcoin and blockchain are tools. Like any tool, their value depends entirely on whether you are using them to solve a real problem you actually have.

For everyday Nigerians and Africans, the real problems are often:

- **Cross-border payments** — sending money to family in another country, or receiving payment from an international client, without losing 10–15% to middlemen and poor rates
- **Dollar access** — holding savings in a currency that does not depreciate as rapidly as the Naira
- **Payments to unbanked friends and family** — especially in rural areas where bank account penetration is low but mobile phones are common
- **Microtransactions** — moving small amounts of money in ways that traditional banking makes slow and expensive

If you have one of these problems, there are blockchain-based solutions worth exploring. If you do not have these problems, Bitcoin may not be the tool for you right now.

## The practical tools

**For dollar savings and stablecoin access:**
Apps like Chipper Cash, Payday, and several others allow Nigerians to hold USDT or USDC — dollar-pegged stablecoins — without needing a US bank account. This is not investment advice; this is an observation about a real use case.

**For cross-border remittances:**
Bitcoin is increasingly being used as a settlement layer for remittances — not because people hold it long-term, but because it is faster and cheaper than traditional wire transfers for corridor payments (e.g., US to Nigeria, UK to Ghana). Apps that abstract the Bitcoin part are usually the most practical entry point.

**For peer-to-peer payments:**
The Lightning Network, built on top of Bitcoin, enables near-instant, near-free micropayments. In Nigeria, applications of Lightning are still early, but the technology is real and the use case is genuine.

## The risks, honestly stated

Bitcoin is volatile. If you hold it as savings and the price drops significantly — which it has, multiple times — the dollar value of your savings drops with it. Stablecoins solve this for savers who want dollar exposure without price volatility.

Scams in the Nigerian crypto space are rampant. If someone is offering you guaranteed returns, a special exchange rate only available through them, or asking you to send crypto to receive more crypto — stop. Leave the conversation. These are scams.

Regulation is evolving. What is legal and accessible today may look different in six months. Build your exposure in ways that can adapt.

## The honest summary

Bitcoin and blockchain are genuinely useful for specific financial problems that many Nigerians face daily. Start with the problem, find the tool designed for that problem, and use established, reputable platforms.

The technology is real. The use cases are real. The risks are also real. Navigate all three with your eyes open.
