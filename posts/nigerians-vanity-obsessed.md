# Nigerians are Vanity obsessed

If you have built a product or offered a service in Nigeria or to a Nigerian, you will realise one indisputable truth: most Nigerians are vanity obsessed.

This is not a condemnation — it is an observation. A pattern that shows up across markets, conversations, and transactions so consistently that it can no longer be dismissed as anecdote.

## What vanity actually means here

Vanity, in this context, is not simply about ego or self-love. It is a deeper compulsion — the need to be seen, to be perceived as successful, capable, and affluent, regardless of the underlying reality. It is the hunger for social proof over substance. The preference for the appearance of value over value itself.

It shows up in how products are purchased. People will stretch budgets they cannot afford to buy the most visible version of a thing. The premium plan. The branded option. The one that *looks* like you have arrived.

It shows up in how businesses are built. An unfortunate number of founders in Nigeria spend disproportionate energy on how their company *appears* — the website aesthetics, the title on the business card, the conference speaking slot — before the product has earned a single loyal user.

It shows up in how progress is measured. Social validation — followers, comments, appearances — often outweighs genuine markers of growth like revenue, retention, or depth of impact.

## Why this happens

The roots are not difficult to trace. Nigeria is a society where scarcity has been normalised across generations. Where the gap between the few who have and the many who do not is wide, visible, and reinforced daily. In environments like this, status signalling becomes a survival mechanism. Being *seen* as successful can unlock real doors — access, trust, opportunity — even when the substance has not yet arrived.

There is also the role of social networks, which have accelerated and monetised the performance of success. Authenticity rarely trends. Aspiration does.

## The product implication

If you are building for this market, you cannot afford to ignore this reality. Vanity is not a bug in your user's psychology — it is a feature. Your product needs to make users feel *elevated*. The UX, the copy, the onboarding, the moment of first value — all of it should make the user feel like they have stepped into a better version of themselves.

The builders who dismiss this as shallow often struggle. The ones who embrace it — who design not just for utility but for social currency — tend to grow.

## The trap

The danger is when vanity becomes internal. When the builder themselves becomes obsessed with the optics of building rather than the act of building. When the pitch deck is more polished than the product. When the LinkedIn post announcing the milestone is crafted before the milestone is real.

That is when vanity stops being a tool and starts being a cage.

---

The market will always tell you the truth eventually. Build something real. But understand the psychology of the people you are building for. In Nigeria, that psychology is rarely purely rational — it is deeply, sometimes beautifully, wrapped in the need to be seen.
