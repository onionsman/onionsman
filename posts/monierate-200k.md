# Monierate hits 200,000 average Monthly visitors one year after launch

What I have learnt in one year of building Monierate as we hit 200,000 monthly traffic, post-revenue and completely bootstrapped.

One year. 200,000 monthly visitors. Zero external funding. One very specific problem that we refused to make complicated.

This is what I have actually learned.

## The problem worth solving is always simple to explain

If you cannot explain what your product does in one sentence to someone who has never heard of it, the problem is either too complex or you have not understood it clearly enough yet.

Monierate's pitch: real-time exchange rate comparisons across Nigerian financial platforms.

That is it. That is the whole thing. The complexity lives in the execution — the data pipelines, the update frequency, the accuracy, the sources — but the problem is one sentence. We never let the complexity of the solution obscure the simplicity of the problem.

## Distribution is not an afterthought

The biggest mistake I see builders make is treating distribution as something that happens after the product is built. It is not. Distribution shapes the product. How your users find you, the language they use when they search for what you do, where they congregate online — this should inform what you build, not just how you market it.

We built Monierate with SEO as a core competency from day one. Not as a checkbox, but as a genuine understanding of how Nigerians search for exchange rate information. That understanding shaped how we structured the site, what data we surfaced, and what content we created around it.

The 200,000 visitors are mostly organic. That does not happen by accident.

## Revenue before scale

We chose to find revenue before pursuing scale. This is a deliberate and sometimes lonely path in a startup ecosystem that often celebrates fundraising as an achievement in itself.

But revenue changes everything. It validates the hypothesis. It creates accountability. It gives you the information you need to know whether you are building something that actually has value, or something that merely has traction.

Post-revenue, we have leverage. We know what users will pay for. We can grow with a clearer head.

## What I would do differently

More user conversations, earlier. Data tells you what is happening. Users tell you why. I relied too heavily on analytics in the first few months and not enough on direct conversation.

Also: launch faster. We were nearly ready two months before we actually launched. Those two months were mostly anxiety dressed up as preparation.

## What comes next

200,000 monthly visitors is a beginning, not an arrival. The work ahead is to deepen the product — to move from a tool people check once to a tool people use as part of how they make financial decisions regularly.

That requires building trust at a much deeper level. And trust, as I have learned, is built in very small increments over a very long time.

One year in. Many more to go.
